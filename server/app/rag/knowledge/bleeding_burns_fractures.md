# Severe bleeding

## Steps
1. Call emergency services for heavy bleeding. Wear gloves or use a plastic bag if possible.
2. Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth or bandage. Do not remove soaked cloths; add more on top.
3. Keep pressure for at least 10 minutes without peeking.
4. For a limb that bleeds life-threateningly and does not stop, apply a tourniquet 5 to 7 cm above the wound (not on a joint), tighten until bleeding stops, and note the time.
5. Lay the person down, raise legs if no leg injury, keep warm. Watch for shock: pale, clammy, fast pulse.

## Nosebleed
Sit leaning forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 to 15 minutes. Do not tilt the head back. Seek care if bleeding lasts longer than 20 minutes.

# Burns

## First aid
1. Stop the burning and remove from the heat source. Remove rings and tight items near the burn before swelling starts.
2. Cool with cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes.
3. Cover loosely with clean non-fluffy cling film or a sterile dressing.
4. Do not apply ice, butter, toothpaste or burst blisters.
5. Seek emergency care for burns larger than the person's palm, on the face, hands, feet, genitals or joints, deep white or charred burns, chemical or electrical burns, and any burn in children or older adults.

# Fractures and sprains

## What to do
Keep the injured area still and supported in the position found. Do not try to straighten it. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for 20 minutes. Call emergency services for suspected neck or back injury, open fractures, a deformed limb, or numb or blue fingers or toes. Use RICE for sprains: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation.

# Head injury

Seek emergency care if there is loss of consciousness, repeated vomiting, confusion, seizure, worsening headache, clear fluid from nose or ears, or unequal pupils. For minor bumps, apply a cold pack and observe for 24 hours.
