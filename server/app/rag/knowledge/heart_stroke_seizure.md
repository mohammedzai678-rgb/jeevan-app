# Heart attack

## Warning signs
Pressure, squeezing or tightness in the chest lasting more than a few minutes or returning. Pain may spread to the arm, jaw, neck, back or stomach. Also shortness of breath, cold sweat, nausea, light-headedness. Women, older adults and people with diabetes may have subtle symptoms such as fatigue or breathlessness.

## What to do
1. Call your local emergency number or press SOS immediately. Do not drive yourself.
2. Sit the person down, loosen tight clothing and keep them calm and still.
3. If not allergic and no doctor has told them to avoid it, chew one regular 300 mg aspirin (or 325 mg).
4. If they have prescribed nitroglycerin, help them take it as directed.
5. If they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, start CPR and use an AED.

# Stroke

## Recognise with BE-FAST
Balance loss, Eyes (sudden vision loss), Face drooping on one side, Arm weakness or numbness, Speech slurred or confused, Time: call emergency services at once. Note the time symptoms started because clot-busting treatment depends on it.

## What to do
Do not give food, drink or medication, including aspirin, because the stroke may be a bleed. Keep the person safe and lying with the head slightly raised. If unconscious but breathing, place in the recovery position.

# Seizure

## First aid
1. Stay calm and note the time. Most seizures stop within 2 to 3 minutes.
2. Clear the area and cushion the head. Do not restrain the person. Never put anything in the mouth.
3. When shaking stops, roll them onto their side (recovery position) and stay until fully alert.
4. Call emergency services if the seizure lasts more than 5 minutes, repeats, the person is injured, pregnant, diabetic, does not wake up, or it is their first seizure.
