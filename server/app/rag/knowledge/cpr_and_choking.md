# CPR (adult)

## When to start CPR
Start CPR if a person is unresponsive and not breathing, or only gasping. Call your local emergency number (112 in India) or press SOS first, and send someone to fetch an AED if one is available.

## Hands-only CPR steps
1. Check the scene is safe. Tap the shoulders and shout. If there is no response and no normal breathing, begin.
2. Place the person flat on a firm surface.
3. Put the heel of one hand on the centre of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), other hand on top, arms straight.
4. Push hard and fast: about 5 cm (2 inches) deep, 100 to 120 compressions per minute. Let the chest fully recoil.
5. Do not stop until help arrives, an AED is ready, or the person starts to move or breathe normally.
6. If trained, give 2 rescue breaths after every 30 compressions.

## Using an AED
Turn it on and follow the voice prompts. Attach the pads to bare chest as pictured, make sure nobody touches the person while it analyses, and press shock only when it says so. Resume compressions immediately afterwards.

## Children and infants
For a child, use one or two hands and compress about one third of the chest depth. For infants use two fingers on the centre of the chest, about 4 cm deep. Give 5 initial rescue breaths if trained, and call for help after 1 minute of CPR if you are alone.

# Choking

## Adult or child over 1 year
1. Ask "Are you choking?" If they can cough, speak or breathe, encourage coughing.
2. If they cannot breathe, cough or speak: give up to 5 firm back blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. Then give up to 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich): stand behind, fist above the navel, pull sharply inward and upward.
4. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 thrusts until the object comes out or the person becomes unresponsive.
5. If unresponsive, call emergency services and start CPR.

## Infant under 1 year
Lay the baby face down along your forearm, head lower than the body, and give 5 back blows. Turn face up and give 5 chest thrusts with two fingers. Never do abdominal thrusts on an infant. Repeat and call for help.
