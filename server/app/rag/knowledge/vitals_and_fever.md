# Understanding your vitals

## Heart rate
Normal resting adult heart rate is 60 to 100 BPM. Athletes may be lower. Above 120 or below 50 at rest deserves attention; above 150 or below 40, or any rate with chest pain, fainting or breathlessness, needs urgent care. Exercise, caffeine, fever, dehydration, anxiety and pain raise heart rate.

## Blood oxygen (SpO2)
Normal is 95 to 100 percent. 94 to 95 is borderline. Below 94 is low and below 90 is an emergency. Readings are unreliable with cold fingers, nail polish, motion or a loose sensor: warm the hand, stay still, and re-measure. Sit upright and breathe slowly while waiting.

## Body temperature
Normal is about 36.1 to 37.2 °C (97 to 99 °F). Fever is 38 °C (100.4 °F) or higher. Above 40 °C (104 °F) is dangerous. Below 35 °C suggests hypothermia.

## Blood pressure
Normal is below 120/80 mmHg. 130/80 or higher is elevated. 140/90 or higher is high. 180/120 or higher is a hypertensive crisis; seek emergency care if accompanied by headache, chest pain, vision changes or weakness. Below 90/60 with dizziness is low.

## ECG from the VitaWatch
The single-lead ECG shows heart rate and rhythm regularity. It is not a full 12-lead ECG. If the ECG rate differs a lot from the pulse sensor, the electrodes may be loose or you may be moving. Leads off means an electrode is detached.

# Fever

## Adults
Rest, drink plenty of fluids, and use paracetamol as directed on the label. Seek care for fever above 39.5 °C, lasting more than 3 days, with stiff neck, confusion, rash, severe headache, breathlessness or in someone who is immunocompromised.

## Children and infants
Any fever of 38 °C or higher in a baby under 3 months needs urgent medical assessment. For older children, seek care for lethargy, refusal to drink, seizures, or fever over 3 days.

# Dehydration and heat illness

## Dehydration
Signs: thirst, dark urine, dry mouth, dizziness, fast heartbeat. Sip oral rehydration solution (ORS) frequently. Seek care for confusion, no urine for 8 hours, or inability to keep fluids down.

## Heat stroke
Body temperature above 40 °C, hot dry or sweaty skin, confusion, fainting. This is an emergency. Call for help, move to shade or AC, remove excess clothing, cool with wet cloths, fans, and ice packs at the neck, armpits and groin.
