# Allergic reactions

## Anaphylaxis
Signs after exposure to food, sting or medicine: swelling of lips, face or throat, wheeze or trouble breathing, hives, vomiting, dizziness or collapse. Use an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector into the outer mid-thigh at once if prescribed, then call emergency services even if symptoms improve. Lie the person flat with legs raised (or sit up if breathing is hard). A second dose can be given after 5 to 15 minutes if no improvement.

## Mild reaction
Hives or itching only: stop the trigger, take a non-drowsy antihistamine, and watch closely for worsening.

# Diabetes emergencies

## Low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)
Shaking, sweating, hunger, confusion, irritability. If conscious and able to swallow, give 15 to 20 g fast sugar (glucose tablets, 150 ml juice or regular soda, 3 teaspoons sugar). Recheck after 15 minutes and repeat if still below 70 mg/dL. Follow with a snack. Never give food or drink to someone who is unconscious; place in recovery position and call emergency services.

## High blood sugar
Very high sugar with extreme thirst, frequent urination, nausea, fruity breath or deep rapid breathing may be ketoacidosis. Seek urgent care.

# Poisoning and overdose

## What to do
Call your local poison helpline or emergency number. In India call 112 or the National Poisons Information Centre. Do not induce vomiting unless instructed. Keep the container or substance label for responders. If the person is unconscious, place them in the recovery position and monitor breathing; start CPR if needed.

# Snake bite

## What to do
1. Move away from the snake and keep the person calm and still.
2. Immobilise the bitten limb at heart level or slightly below. Remove rings and tight items.
3. Do not cut, suck, apply ice or a tight tourniquet. Do not give alcohol.
4. Get to a hospital with anti-venom immediately. Note the time and snake appearance, but do not try to catch it.

# Drowning and electric shock

## Drowning
Pull the person out without endangering yourself, call emergency services and start CPR with rescue breaths if not breathing.

## Electric shock
Do not touch the person until the power is switched off at the source. Check breathing, start CPR if needed, and treat burns. Always seek hospital care after a significant shock.
